# Paper outline

## 1. Introduction

- Distinguish correlation, conditional predictability and causality.
- Motivate why network novelty need not imply forecast improvement.
- State contributions and the admissibility of a negative result.

## 2. Related literature

- HAR and realized-volatility forecasting.
- Financial and temporal networks.
- Nonlinear predictive dependence and group importance.
- Forecast comparison under dependent loss differentials.

## 3. Data and point-in-time universe

- MOEX 10-minute candles and strict session QC.
- All-82 history versus dynamic top-40 evaluation universe.
- Temporal segments and the 2022 break.
- IMOEX factor and causal residual realized variance.

## 4. Methods

- Train / importance / forecast clocks.
- Pearson, ElasticNet and CatBoost edge definitions.
- Current and dynamic graph features.
- HAR model family M0–M8 and placebos.
- QLIKE, calibration, HAC, block bootstrap and Holm adjustment.

## 5. Development evidence

- Common-sample forecast table.
- Structural comparison and reciprocity.
- Temporal stability and current-versus-dynamic increment.
- Residual graph topology and forecast result.
- Concentration, prediction-floor and robust-linear diagnostics.

## 6. Confirmatory design

- Development cutoff and stopping rule.
- v10 historical freeze and v10.1 prospective amendment.
- Sealed prediction workflow and immutable manifests.

## 7. Discussion

- Strong own-volatility persistence dominates weak network signal.
- Differencing two noisy graph estimates reduces signal-to-noise ratio.
- Interchangeable correlated sources reduce edge-set stability.
- Predictive asymmetry does not identify causal transmission.

## 8. Limitations and conclusion

- One market, modest effective time-series sample and estimated graph error.
- No claim of robust forecasting superiority yet.
- Generalization and future confirmatory evidence.
